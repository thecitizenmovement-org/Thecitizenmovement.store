YOUR RIGHTS IN POLICE ENCOUNTERS
The Citizen Movement -- thecitizenmovement.org

Knowing your rights in the moment can change the outcome of your case.
Read this now. Memorize the short phrases. Stay calm.

IF YOU ARE STOPPED

- You may ask: "Am I free to leave?" If the officer says yes, you may
  calmly walk away.
- If you are not free to leave, you are being detained. Stay calm and
  do not argue.
- You have the right to remain silent. You do not have to answer
  questions about where you are going, what you are doing, or who you
  know.
- You do not have to consent to a search of yourself, your belongings,
  your car, or your home. Say so clearly (see phrases below).

IF YOU ARE ARRESTED

- Use your right to remain silent -- actually use it. Silence cannot be
  used against you. Talking can.
- Ask for a lawyer clearly: "I want a lawyer." Then say nothing else
  until one is present.
- Do not resist physically, even if you believe the arrest is unlawful.
  Resisting adds charges. Fight an unlawful arrest in court, not on the
  street.
- Do not consent to searches. Do not answer questions about the
  incident.

SEARCHES

- Police generally need a warrant, your consent, or a legal exception
  (such as an arrest or something in plain view) to search you, your
  car, or your home.
- If an officer asks to search, you may refuse. Say: "I do not consent
  to a search."
- If the officer searches anyway, do not physically resist. Say the
  phrase clearly so it is on record, then challenge the search later
  in court.
- Never lie to an officer about your identity in a way that creates a
  new charge -- but you are not required to volunteer information.

WHAT TO SAY -- MEMORIZE THESE

- "I am exercising my right to remain silent."
- "I do not consent to a search."
- "Am I free to leave?"
- "I want a lawyer."

DOCUMENT EVERYTHING

- Badge numbers, officer names, patrol car numbers.
- Date, time, and exact location.
- Names and phone numbers of witnesses.
- If it is safe, record video. You have the right to observe and
  record police in public.
- Write down everything you remember as soon as possible afterward.

This fact sheet provides general legal information only -- not legal advice. Consult a licensed attorney for your situation. -- The Citizen Movement, thecitizenmovement.org
