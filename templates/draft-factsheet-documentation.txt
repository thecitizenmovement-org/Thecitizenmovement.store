WHAT COUNTS: A GUIDE TO DOCUMENTATION THAT HOLDS UP
The Citizen Movement -- thecitizenmovement.org

Cases are won on paper, not on stories. The side with the better
documents usually wins. This sheet ranks what counts, what is weak,
and what will destroy your credibility.

WHY THIS MATTERS

- Judges and juries believe what they can see and verify. Your word
  alone is the weakest evidence in the room.
- Every strong case is a stack of documents in chronological order.
  That stack starts today, with what you preserve right now.
- Bad documentation is worse than none. One altered photo or
  backdated letter can destroy your credibility on everything else.

TOP TIER -- STRONGEST

- Certified court records. Stamped, certified copies from the clerk.
  The gold standard.
- Government and agency records obtained through CPRA or FOIA
  requests. Official, dated, and hard to dispute.
- Sworn declarations under penalty of perjury (CCP 2015.5 -- no
  notary needed in California). A witness's signed, dated statement
  of personal knowledge.
- Contemporaneous written records: notes, logs, and emails written
  AT THE TIME of the event, not reconstructed months later. Date
  stamps are everything.
- Authenticated audio and video with intact metadata. Original files,
  unedited, with date/time data preserved.
- Business records: medical files, bills, receipts, pay stubs. Made
  in the ordinary course of business, not for your case.

MID TIER -- USEFUL BUT NEEDS SUPPORT

- Photos and video without clear dates. Fix them: attach a signed
  declaration stating when and where each was taken and by whom.
- Emails and text messages. Save FULL threads, not screenshots of
  fragments. Fragments look cherry-picked. Export or forward complete
  conversations.
- Social media posts. Capture the URL and the date, not just a
  screenshot. Screenshots alone get challenged -- the link and
  timestamp back them up.
- Witness statements not yet sworn. Useful now, but get them into
  signed declarations under penalty of perjury as soon as possible.
  An unsworn "he told me" is far weaker than a declaration.

LOW TIER -- WEAK ALONE

- Your memory of what happened, with nothing backing it. Honest, but
  uncorroborated. Pair it with anything -- a date, a receipt, a
  witness -- and it climbs.
- Undated handwritten notes made long after the event. Better than
  nothing, but courts discount reconstruction.
- What someone told you someone else said. This is hearsay, and
  courts generally exclude it. Get the statement from the original
  speaker or leave it out.
- Anonymous or unsigned claims. Without a name and a signature under
  penalty of perjury, they carry almost no weight.

NOT VALID -- DO NOT USE

- Altered or edited documents or photos. If you are caught with one,
  your credibility on EVERYTHING is gone. Never alter, crop out
  context, or "clean up" evidence.
- Backdated anything. Writing an earlier date on something created
  later is dishonest and discoverable. Date things when you make them.
- Speculation presented as fact. "He must have known" is not a fact.
  Label what you KNOW separately from what you BELIEVE. Guessing in
  a sworn statement is dangerous.
- Anything obtained illegally. Stolen records, hacked accounts, and
  unlawful recordings can be excluded and can create liability for
  you.

PRESERVATION RULES

- Erase nothing. Keep originals untouched and work from copies.
- Never edit original photos or videos. Export or copy them, and
  edit only the copies.
- Back up everything in two places: your device plus cloud storage,
  a drive, or a trusted person's copies.
- Keep envelopes for mailed items. Postmarks prove dates.
- Write the date received on everything that arrives, the day it
  arrives.
- Log every handoff: who got what, when, and how. A simple notebook
  line -- "Gave copy of grievance to Sgt. [name], [date]" -- is a
  chain of custody.

THE GOLDEN RULE

- If it is not written down, dated, and preserved, it barely exists.
- Document the day it happens, not the day you need it. By the time
  you need it, the details are gone and the proof is cold.
- Your future self, standing in front of a judge, will thank you for
  every dated page you kept.
