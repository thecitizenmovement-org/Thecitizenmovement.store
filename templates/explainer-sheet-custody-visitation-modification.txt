EXPLAINER SHEET — MODIFYING CHILD CUSTODY / VISITATION (PARENTING TIME)
CALIFORNIA — USE THE OFFICIAL JUDICIAL COUNCIL FORMS, DO NOT USE A
SUBSTITUTE FORM
THE CITIZEN MOVEMENT — GENERAL LEGAL INFORMATION ONLY, NOT LEGAL ADVICE

OFFICIAL FORMS — USE THESE, NOT A RECREATED FORM:
- FL-300, Request for Order — the moving document. Use the existing
  case number; check the boxes for child custody and for
  visitation / parenting time.
- FL-311, Child Custody and Visitation (Parenting Time)
  Application Attachment — use it to lay out precisely the orders
  wanted: legal custody, physical custody, weekly schedule,
  holidays, transportation, and exchange location.
- FL-320, Responsive Declaration to Request for Order — the other
  parent's response.
- Get all forms from the California Courts website (courts.ca.gov)
  and the court clerk in the county where the prior order was
  issued. This sheet gives the CONTENT for the declaration that
  supports the FL-300 — it does not replace the forms.

LEGAL VS PHYSICAL CUSTODY:
- Legal custody: who makes decisions about health, education, and
  welfare.
- Physical custody: where the child lives, and the parenting-time
  schedule. "Visitation" is now usually called parenting time.

THE TWO STANDARDS — DO NOT CONFUSE THEM:
1. Best interest of the child — ALWAYS applies. Family Code 3011:
   the child's health, safety, and welfare; any history of abuse;
   the nature and amount of contact with each parent; habitual or
   continued use of illegal drugs or abuse of alcohol by a parent.
   Family Code 3020 states the policy that children have frequent
   and continuing contact with both parents when consistent with
   the child's safety.
2. Significant change of circumstances — ADDITIONALLY required to
   change a FINAL custody order (Montenegro v. Diaz (2001)
   26 Cal.4th 249). The moving parent must show something
   significant has changed since the last order, and that a new
   arrangement is essential to the child's welfare. Stability for
   the child is the reason for this rule.
   - Passage of time alone, general dissatisfaction, or re-arguing
     facts the judge already considered does not meet this test.
   - Temporary or interim orders are different: they can generally
     be changed on best interest alone.
   - A change to a parenting-time schedule alone is generally held
     to a lower standard than changing the primary custodial parent
     — best interest, rather than the full significant-change test
     for a final custody label. Check current case law before
     relying on this distinction in your case.

WHAT COUNTS AS A SIGNIFICANT CHANGE (EXAMPLES, NOT A CHECKLIST):
- A parent relocating, or planning to move
- A major change in work schedule, housing, or availability
- Ongoing interference with parenting time
- A child's evolving needs, schedule, or age-appropriate preference
- A parent's illness or disability, or a safety/well-being concern
In every case, the change must affect the CHILD, not only the
parents, and must be proved with facts, not conclusions.

DECLARATION CONTENT GUIDE — WHAT TO WRITE (FOR THE FL-300
SUPPORTING DECLARATION, UNDER PENALTY OF PERJURY):
1. Current order: date, court, and what it orders now (legal and
   physical custody, schedule).
2. What has changed since that order: numbered facts, with dates,
   documents, and witnesses — one fact per paragraph.
3. Why the change matters to the child: tie each changed fact to
   health, safety, welfare, or contact under Family Code 3011.
4. Exactly what you are asking for: the full new schedule from
   FL-311 — weekdays, weekends, holidays, transportation, exchange
   location — so the judge can grant it as written.
5. What you have done to resolve it without court: communications,
   mediation attempts, and any agreement — and why it did not work.

PROCESS IN OUTLINE:
1. File FL-300 + FL-311 (+ declaration) in the court that issued
   the current order.
2. Serve the other parent as the FL-300 instructions and local
   rules require (one practitioner guide puts service at 16 days
   before the hearing — verify against your court's instructions;
   do not rely on that number alone).
3. Attend Child Custody Recommending Counseling (CCRC, formerly
   mediation) if the parents cannot agree — Family Code 3170
   generally requires it before a judge decides contested custody
   or visitation.
4. Hearing: both sides are heard; the judge decides on best
   interest, and, for a final order, on significant change as well.
5. A verbal agreement or private written agreement between parents
   does NOT alter the existing court order. Only a court order
   changes it.

WARNINGS:
- Do not file on the same facts the judge already decided — it
  gets the same result and may draw a fee request.
- Do not use a custody filing to relitigate a ruling you disagree
  with — that is an appeal or a different motion, not a custody
  modification.
- Family-law cases turn heavily on local rules, CCRC practice, and
  the specific county's forms. Consult a licensed family-law
  attorney before filing, especially where abuse, relocation, or a
  restraining order is involved.
