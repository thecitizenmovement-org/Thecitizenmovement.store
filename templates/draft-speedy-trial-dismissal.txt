MOTION TO DISMISS FOR VIOLATION OF SPEEDY-TRIAL RIGHTS
CALIFORNIA PENAL CODE SECTION 1382; SERNA MOTION
THE CITIZEN MOVEMENT — GENERAL LEGAL INFORMATION ONLY, NOT LEGAL ADVICE

INSTRUCTIONS: California has TWO speedy-trial protections, and this
template covers both — plead them separately and do not confuse
them. (1) STATUTORY: Penal Code 1382 sets hard post-arraignment
deadlines; if trial does not begin in time and no good cause or
valid time waiver exists, the court must dismiss. (2)
CONSTITUTIONAL: a Serna motion (Serna v. Superior Court (1985))
challenges unreasonable delay between accusation/arrest and trial
under the Sixth Amendment and California Constitution article I,
section 15, and requires a showing of unreasonable delay plus
prejudice. WARNING: Waiving time — expressly or by requesting or
consenting to a later date — can defeat a Section 1382 motion. Do
not waive time casually, and check the minute orders for every prior
waiver or continuance before filing. Consult a licensed attorney;
a denied Serna motion may be reviewable by writ, not only by appeal.

STATUTORY DEADLINES — PENAL CODE 1382 (CHECK YOUR CASE TYPE):
- Felony: trial within 60 calendar days of arraignment on the
  indictment or information.
- Misdemeanor / infraction, defendant IN custody: trial within 30
  calendar days of arraignment or plea, whichever is later.
- Misdemeanor / infraction, defendant OUT of custody: trial within
  45 calendar days of arraignment or plea, whichever is later.
Exceptions: good cause for delay, or a valid time waiver by the
defendant, or a continuance the defendant requested or consented to.
