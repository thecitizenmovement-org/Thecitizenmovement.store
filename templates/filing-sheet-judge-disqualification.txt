ASSISTANT FILING SHEET — JUDGE DISQUALIFICATION (CCP 170.1 / 170.6)
For the AI assistant to present to a paying customer alongside
draft-judge-disqualification.txt. General information only, not
legal advice.

WHAT THIS IS FOR:
Removing a judge from a case — either because a legal ground for
disqualification exists (for cause), or, without any reason, by a
one-time peremptory challenge.

WHO FILES IT:
A party to the case, or the party's attorney. In a criminal case,
the defendant (or defense counsel) files it in the trial court
where the case is pending.

WHICH ROUTE — DECIDE FIRST:
1. Peremptory (CCP 170.6) — use this FIRST if the window is still
   open. No reason, no proof, sworn statement only. One per side
   per case. If it is already spent or the window has passed, it
   is gone.
2. For cause (CCP 170.1 / 170.3) — use this when a specific ground
   exists and can be proved with facts: personal knowledge, prior
   lawyer role, financial interest, family relationship, or a
   reasonable doubt about impartiality / actual bias.

DEADLINES — THE FILING FAILS ON TIMING MORE THAN ON ANYTHING ELSE:
- Peremptory: at least 5 days before trial/hearing if the judge
  was known at least 10 days before; 10 days (criminal) / 15 days
  (civil) after notice of an all-purpose assignment; 60 days after
  notice if the same judge is reassigned after a reversal for a
  new trial. Never after trial commences.
- For cause: file promptly once the disqualifying facts are known.
  A statement filed after trial/hearing has commenced is handled
  differently and may not stop the proceeding while another judge
  decides it.

WHAT GOES IN IT:
- For cause: caption, judge name, the exact CCP 170.1(a)
  subdivision, and numbered facts — dates, words, rulings,
  documents — verified under penalty of perjury. Conclusions
  ("the judge is biased") without facts will be stricken.
- Peremptory: caption, judge name, and the sworn statement that
  the judge is prejudiced and a fair trial cannot be had before
  that judge. Nothing more is required.

WHAT TO ATTACH / SERVE:
- Exhibits supporting the for-cause facts, where available.
- Proof of service on all parties, and service on the challenged
  judge or the judge's clerk as the statute and local rules
  require.

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER FILING:
- For cause: the judge has 10 days after filing or service,
  whichever is later, to consent or file a verified answer. No
  answer in time is deemed consent. If answered and disputed,
  another judge decides — never the challenged judge.
- Peremptory: if timely and in proper form, disqualification is
  automatic and the truth of the affidavit cannot be contested.

COMMON TRAPS:
- Pleading conclusions instead of facts (for cause).
- Filing a second for-cause statement on the same facts.
- Burning the single peremptory challenge early, or missing its
  window and then having no route left except for cause.
- Filing a peremptory challenge after trial has commenced.

OFFICIAL FORMS / SOURCES TO CHECK:
- No Judicial Council form is the substitute for the verified
  statement itself — the statement is drafted, not a fill-in form.
  Check CCP 170.1, 170.3, 170.4, 170.6 at California Legislative
  Information / Justia, and the court's local rules for assignment
  type, service, and filing method.
