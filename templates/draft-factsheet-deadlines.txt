DEADLINES THAT CAN KILL YOUR CASE
The Citizen Movement -- thecitizenmovement.org

The legal system runs on deadlines. Miss one and your case can die no
matter how strong your facts are. Learn these. Calendar them.

GOVERNMENT TORT CLAIM -- 6 MONTHS

- Before you can sue a city, county, or other public agency for money,
  you MUST file a claim with that agency within 6 MONTHS of the
  incident.
- This is a hard deadline. One day late and the claim is barred.
- See our free "Claim Against a Public Entity" template for the form.
- After you file, the agency has 45 days to respond. If it does
  nothing, the claim is deemed rejected -- then you have 6 months from
  rejection to file your lawsuit.

POLICE COMPLAINT -- FILE PROMPTLY

- File misconduct complaints with the agency's Internal Affairs or
  civilian review board as soon as possible.
- California law generally gives agencies 1 YEAR from the incident to
  complete investigations that could lead to officer discipline.
- Delay can kill accountability even when a lawsuit deadline has not
  passed.

CPRA REQUESTS -- 10 DAYS

- An agency must respond to your California Public Records Act
  request within 10 days.
- If denied, appeal fast with our free "CPRA Denial Appeal" template.
- If the agency stalls, the paper trail of your request and their
  silence is evidence.

TOLLING -- WHEN THE CLOCK PAUSES

- Sometimes the deadline clock stops. This is called tolling.
- The clock may pause while the injured person is a minor, is
  incapacitated, or while the wrongdoer has fled or concealed their
  identity.
- Military service pauses civil deadlines under federal law.
- Courts can also pause deadlines for extraordinary circumstances
  outside your control (equitable tolling) -- but never count on it.
  File on time.

PAPER TRAIL RULE

- Every deadline you meet should leave proof: a date-stamped copy
  from the clerk, a certified mail receipt, or an email confirmation.
- Your proof is your power. No receipt means it is your word against
  theirs.

CALENDAR EVERYTHING

- The day you learn of an incident, write down every deadline that
  applies and put each on a calendar with a reminder two weeks early.
- Deadlines are the cheapest thing in law to get right and the most
  expensive to get wrong.
