ARRESTED: WHAT HAPPENS NEXT
The Citizen Movement -- thecitizenmovement.org

An arrest is the start of a process with strict timelines. Knowing the
steps keeps you from being lost in the system.

BOOKING

- After arrest you are taken to jail for booking: fingerprints, photo,
  personal information, and a search of your belongings.
- Booking can take a few hours or most of a day depending on how busy
  the facility is.
- Your belongings are inventoried and stored. Get the property receipt
  and keep the number.

YOUR FIRST COURT DATE -- ARRAIGNMENT

- You must be brought before a judge usually within 48 hours of
  arrest, not counting weekends and holidays.
- At arraignment the judge tells you the charges, you enter a plea
  (guilty, not guilty, or no contest), and bail is addressed.
- If you cannot afford a lawyer, tell the judge -- a public defender
  will be appointed.
- Plead NOT GUILTY at arraignment unless your lawyer advises
  otherwise. You can change your plea later. You cannot easily undo a
  guilty plea.

BAIL

- Bail is money or property posted to get out of jail while your case
  is pending. It is returned when you show up to court.
- Each county has a bail schedule setting amounts by charge.
- Ask the judge for release on your own recognizance (OR) -- no money
  required -- especially if you have ties to the community, a job, or
  family here.
- Tell the judge: where you live, how long you have lived there, your
  job, and that you will attend every court date.

DO THIS IMMEDIATELY

- Call a lawyer or ask for the public defender. Say nothing about your
  case until you have one.
- Tell someone outside where you are and which facility.
- Write down everything you remember about the arrest: who, what,
  when, where, witnesses. Do this now -- memory fades.
- Do NOT discuss your case on jail phones. Jail calls are recorded
  (except calls to your attorney) and prosecutors can use them.
- Do not discuss your case with other incarcerated people either.

TIMELINE AFTER ARRAIGNMENT

- Misdemeanor: trial generally within 30 to 45 days of arraignment if
  you are in custody, longer if you are out.
- Felony: a preliminary hearing is usually held within 10 court days
  of arraignment, where a judge decides if there is enough evidence
  for trial.
- You have a constitutional right to a speedy trial. Talk to your
  lawyer before agreeing to delay it.
