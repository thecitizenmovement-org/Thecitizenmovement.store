BUILDING YOUR CASE TIMELINE: DEFENSE AND OFFENSE
The Citizen Movement -- thecitizenmovement.org

Whether you are defending yourself or going after someone who harmed
you, your timeline is your weapon. Here is how to build one and how
to use it.

WHY A TIMELINE WINS

- Memory fades. Paper does not. A written timeline keeps your facts
  straight months later when details blur.
- Judges, juries, and investigators think in chronology. "What
  happened, in what order?" is always the first question.
- A timeline exposes lies and gaps. When the other side's story does
  not fit the dates, the timeline shows it.
- It keeps YOU honest. If your own dates do not add up, better to find
  out now than in front of a judge.

HOW TO BUILD ONE

- One row per event. Each row needs: date, time (if known), what
  happened, the source or proof, and any witnesses.
- Put events in chronological order, earliest first.
- Number the rows. You will refer to them by number later ("see
  timeline row 14").
- Include the bad facts too. Hiding a bad date or a damaging event
  always hurts you worse later -- the other side will find it. Your
  own timeline should be the most complete version of the story.
- Include non-events: the call that was never returned, the request
  that got no answer. "No response by [date]" is a fact.
- Start with what you remember, then fill gaps from records: emails,
  texts, court papers, receipts, photos with dates.

THE CLOCKS TO PUT ON YOUR TIMELINE

Mark every legal deadline directly on the timeline so you can see
them against the events. The big ones in California:

- CPRA REQUEST RESPONSE -- 10 DAYS. An agency must respond to a
  California Public Records Act request within 10 days.
- CRIMINAL DISCOVERY -- 15 DAYS. After your informal discovery request
  under Penal Code 1054.5, the prosecution has 15 days to turn over
  the material.
- GOVERNMENT TORT CLAIM -- 6 MONTHS. You must file a claim against a
  public entity within 6 months of the incident date. Miss it and your
  right to sue is barred. This is the deadline that kills the most
  cases.
- POLICE MISCONDUCT INVESTIGATION -- generally 1 YEAR for matters
  that could lead to officer discipline. File complaints promptly.
- ARRAIGNMENT -- within 48 HOURS of arrest, excluding weekends and
  holidays. Your first court appearance comes fast.
- SPEEDY TRIAL -- misdemeanor and felony cases each have speedy-trial
  rights. Know yours or ask your lawyer.
- STATUTES OF LIMITATIONS -- these vary by claim. Look up the one for
  YOUR claim. Do not guess. Do not assume.

TOLLING -- WHEN THE CLOCK PAUSES

- Tolling pauses the countdown on a statute of limitations. The clock
  stops, then resumes when the condition ends.
- Common tolling grounds: the injured person was under 18 (minority);
  mental incapacity; the defendant fled the state or concealed their
  identity; military service (Servicemembers Civil Relief Act);
  equitable tolling when extraordinary circumstances blocked filing
  despite diligent effort (such as fraudulent concealment).
- Honest note: tolling is ARGUED, not automatic. A judge decides
  whether it applies. Never rely on tolling as your plan -- file on
  time and argue tolling only as a backup.

USING IT ON DEFENSE

- Find alibi windows. Your timeline shows where you were at every
  key moment. If the accusation does not fit your timeline, say so
  with the proof attached.
- Expose inconsistencies. When the other side's dates shift between
  statements, line up their versions against your timeline and show
  the contradictions.
- Show you acted promptly. A timeline proving you filed, complained,
  or responded immediately undercuts claims that you sat on your
  rights.

USING IT ON OFFENSE

- Show a pattern. One incident is a dispute. Five incidents on a
  timeline is a pattern of conduct.
- Prove notice: "they knew and did nothing." Mark the date you
  complained and every date after it when the misconduct continued.
  Each post-complaint event strengthens your case.
- Stack damages over time. Medical bills, lost wages, and harms
  accumulate visibly on a timeline. Totals at the end hit harder.

KEEP IT CURRENT

- Add every new event, every filing, every response the day it
  happens -- not later.
- When you mail something, add the mailing date AND the certified
  mail number.
- When a deadline passes, mark what happened: met, missed, or
  extended.
- Your timeline is a living document. A stale timeline is a dead one.
