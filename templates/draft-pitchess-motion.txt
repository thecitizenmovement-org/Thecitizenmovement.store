PITCHESS MOTION — DISCOVERY OF PEACE OFFICER PERSONNEL RECORDS
CALIFORNIA EVIDENCE CODE SECTIONS 1043-1045; PENAL CODE 832.7-832.8
THE CITIZEN MOVEMENT — GENERAL LEGAL INFORMATION ONLY, NOT LEGAL ADVICE

INSTRUCTIONS: A Pitchess motion asks the court to review a peace or
custodial officer's personnel records in chambers and disclose only
records relevant to the pending case. It is a discovery motion in a
pending criminal case, not a public-records request and not a way to
obtain an officer's full file. The good-cause affidavit is the most
important part — without it, the court will not reach in-camera
review. File in the criminal case, serve the agency that holds the
records, and follow the criminal court's local rules and hearing
schedule. Consult a licensed attorney; defense counsel ordinarily
prepares the good-cause declaration.

STATUTE: Evidence Code 1043(b) — the written motion must include:
(1) identification of the proceeding, the party seeking discovery,
the officer whose records are sought, the agency with custody and
control, and the hearing time and place; (2) a description of the
type of records sought; (3) affidavits showing good cause, setting
forth materiality to the pending litigation and stating upon
reasonable belief that the agency has the records. Evidence Code
1045 — if good cause is shown, the court examines the records in
chambers (in camera) and excludes, among other things, complaints
more than five years old, investigators' conclusions, and facts so
remote as to be of little practical benefit.

GOOD CAUSE — TWO PARTS, BOTH REQUIRED:
1. MATERIALITY: Propose a defense, and explain how the records sought
   could lead to relevant evidence or admissible direct or
   impeachment evidence supporting that defense. A scenario of
   officer misconduct that could or might have occurred is enough
   at this stage — you need not prove the misconduct happened.
2. REASONABLE BELIEF: State, on reasonable belief (which may rest on
   rational inference), that the agency identified has the type of
   records sought. You need not prove a particular record exists.

MOTION — FILL IN:
Defendant moves for an order requiring [agency name], custodian of
records for Officer [name, badge/ID if known], to produce for
in-camera review: [describe the type of records sought — e.g.,
"prior citizen complaints alleging dishonesty, false reports, or
excessive force," with a time period]. Hearing date/time/department:
[___]. Notice to the agency and proof of service are attached.

DECLARATION SHOWING GOOD CAUSE — FILL IN:
I, [declarant name, role], declare:
1. The pending charges are [charges, case number].
2. The defense is [proposed defense, in plain terms].
3. The facts supporting the defense scenario are [specific facts:
   what the defendant says happened, with dates and places].
4. Records of the type described above, if they exist, would be
   material because [how they could support the defense or impeach
   the officer's account].
5. On reasonable belief, [agency] has custody of such records
   because [basis for belief: the agency employs the officer, keeps
   personnel/complaint files under Penal Code 832.5, etc.].
I declare under penalty of perjury that the foregoing is true and
correct. Signed: [Name, date, place].

REQUESTED ORDER:
Defendant asks the court to find good cause, conduct an in-camera
review under Evidence Code 1045, and disclose all relevant records
and the names/addresses of complainants and witnesses for disclosed
complaints, as the statute allows.
