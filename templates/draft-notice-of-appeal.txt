CALIFORNIA AND FEDERAL APPEALS
THE CITIZEN MOVEMENT — GENERAL LEGAL INFORMATION ONLY, NOT LEGAL ADVICE

INSTRUCTIONS: A notice of appeal is a short, jurisdictional filing —
it starts the appeal, it is not the argument. File it in the TRIAL
court (superior court in California, district court in federal
court), not directly in the appellate court, unless the rules for
your case say otherwise. Use the court's own notice-of-appeal form
where one exists (California Judicial Council forms APP-002 for
unlimited civil, CR-120 for felony criminal, and the appellate
division forms for limited civil / misdemeanor cases; federal courts
use FRAP Form 1). This template organizes what goes in that form
and what comes next. Deadlines below are jurisdictional — a late
notice generally cannot be cured, so calculate your deadline the
day judgment is entered and do not rely on this summary alone.
Check the current California Rules of Court, Federal Rules of
Appellate Procedure, and the appellate court's local rules before
filing. Consult a licensed attorney; appeals have strict briefing
and record rules that this notice alone does not satisfy.

DEADLINES — VERIFY AGAINST YOUR JUDGMENT AND SERVICE:
California, unlimited civil (CRC 8.104): the earliest of — 60 days
after the superior court clerk serves a Notice of Entry of judgment
or a file-stamped copy of the judgment; 60 days after a party serves
a Notice of Entry or file-stamped copy with proof of service; or
180 days after entry of judgment if no such service occurred.
Timely post-judgment motions (new trial, JNOV, vacate, reconsider)
may extend the time under CRC 8.108 — check that rule, it never
shortens the period.
California, limited civil (CRC 8.822): 30 days after clerk or party
service of Notice of Entry, or 90 days after entry if no service.
California, criminal felony (CRC 8.308): 60 days after rendition of
judgment or making of the appealable order.
California, criminal misdemeanor/infraction (CRC 8.853): 30 days
after rendition of judgment or making of the appealable order.
Federal, civil (FRAP 4(a)): 30 days after entry of judgment or order;
60 days if the United States, a federal agency, or a federal officer
or employee sued in an official capacity is a party. Certain timely
post-judgment motions toll the period under FRAP 4(a)(4).
Federal, criminal defendant (FRAP 4(b)): 14 days after the later of
entry of judgment/order or the government's notice of appeal.
Federal, government criminal appeal (FRAP 4(b)): 30 days after the
later of entry of judgment/order or a defendant's notice of appeal.

[Your name], Appellant, v. [Opposing party name(s)], Respondent.
Trial court: [Superior Court of California, County of ___ / United
States District Court for the ___ District of California], Case No.
[___].
NOTICE IS HEREBY GIVEN that [Appellant name] appeals to the
[California Court of Appeal, ___ District / Appellate Division of
the Superior Court / United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth
Circuit] from the following judgment / order:
- Judgment/order: [describe it — e.g., "Judgment entered [date]"]
- Date entered: [date]
- Date Notice of Entry served, and by whom, if any: [date / party]
- Grounds in brief (notice only — argument goes in the brief):
  [e.g., "The trial court erred in ___"]
Appellant requests preparation of the record on appeal as follows:
[Designate: clerk's transcript / reporter's transcript / agreed
statement, as the appellate rules require — check the designation
deadline and fee/waiver rules for your court.]
Fee / waiver: [Filing fee paid / fee waiver or in forma pauperis
application filed — California: fee waiver forms FW-001 et seq.;
federal: 28 U.S.C. 1915.]
Signed: [Name, date, address, phone, email]. Proof of service on all
parties attached.

WHAT COMES AFTER THE NOTICE — DO NOT STOP HERE:
1. Record on appeal: designate and pay for / waive the record;
   the trial court clerk transmits it to the appellate court.
2. Opening brief: statement of the case, statement of facts with
   record citations, argument (one heading per issue, with the
   standard of review and the authority for each), and conclusion
   stating the exact relief requested (reverse, vacate, remand).
   Brief length, format, and service rules are set by the appellate
   rules (California CRC Title 8, Division 1; FRAP 28, 31, 32) and
   the court's local rules — check them before drafting.
3. Respondent's brief, then optional reply brief, then oral argument
   if requested and calendared.
4. A CPRA order is NOT appealed this way — see the CPRA enforcement
   template: a CPRA order is reviewable by writ petition under
   Government Code 7923.500, not by ordinary appeal.

COMMON DISMISSAL TRAPS:
- Late notice (jurisdictional).
- Appealing a non-appealable interlocutory order.
- Failing to designate or pay for the record.
- Filing the notice in the appellate court instead of the trial
  court, where the rules require trial-court filing.
