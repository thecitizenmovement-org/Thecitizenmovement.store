MOTION FOR SUBSTITUTION OF APPOINTED COUNSEL
(Marsden Motion -- People v. Marsden (1970) 2 Cal.3d 118)

INSTRUCTIONS: Use this motion when your court-appointed attorney is not
representing you adequately and the attorney-client relationship has
broken down. File the original plus 2 copies with the court clerk and
serve a copy on the district attorney. Do NOT give a copy to your
attorney -- the judge will handle that at the hearing. Be respectful in
court. Stick to specific facts, not insults.

REQUEST

I, [YOUR FULL NAME], the defendant in the above case, respectfully ask
this Court to discharge my currently appointed counsel, [ATTORNEY'S
NAME], and to appoint new counsel to represent me. Under People v.
Marsden, I have the right to state the reasons for my request on the
record, and the Court must hold a hearing on this motion.

WHAT TO EXPECT AT THE HEARING

The judge will clear the courtroom -- the prosecutor and spectators
leave, and only you, your attorney, and the judge remain. The judge
will ask YOU to speak first: state your reasons calmly and stick to
the facts in this motion. Then your attorney gets to respond. Do not
interrupt. The judge may deny the motion, grant it and appoint new
counsel, or take other action. If denied, remain respectful -- you can
raise the issue again later if things get worse, and the denial is
part of the record for appeal.

FILING NOTES: File the original plus 2 copies with the clerk of the
superior court hearing your criminal case. Serve one copy on the
district attorney's office (the clerk can tell you the procedure).
Keep one stamped copy for yourself. File as soon as the problems
arise -- do not wait until the week of trial, or the judge may deny
the motion as untimely.
