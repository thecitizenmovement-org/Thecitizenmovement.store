STATEMENT OF DISQUALIFICATION OF JUDGE — FOR CAUSE
AND PEREMPTORY CHALLENGE (AFFIDAVIT OF PREJUDICE)
CALIFORNIA CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE SECTIONS 170.1, 170.3, 170.6
THE CITIZEN MOVEMENT — GENERAL LEGAL INFORMATION ONLY, NOT LEGAL ADVICE

INSTRUCTIONS: California has TWO different judge challenges, and
this template covers both — use only the one that fits, and do not
mix them. (1) FOR CAUSE (CCP 170.1 / 170.3): you must state specific
facts, verified under penalty of perjury, showing a legal ground
for disqualification. Conclusions alone ("the judge is biased")
will get the statement stricken on its face under CCP 170.4(b).
(2) PEREMPTORY (CCP 170.6): no reason or proof is required — only
a sworn statement that you believe the judge is prejudiced and you
cannot have a fair and impartial trial before that judge — but you
get only ONE per side per case, and the timing is strict. If the
peremptory window has passed, for-cause is the remaining route.
Check the current CCP text and your court's local rules before
filing. Consult a licensed attorney; a failed or stricken challenge
can leave you before the same judge, and a peremptory challenge,
once used, is spent.

PART A — FOR-CAUSE STATEMENT OF DISQUALIFICATION (CCP 170.1, 170.3)

GROUNDS (CCP 170.1(a)) — CHECK ONLY THE GROUND(S) YOU CAN SUPPORT
WITH FACTS:
(1) Personal knowledge of disputed evidentiary facts, or the judge,
    spouse, or a person within the third degree of relationship to
    either is likely to be a material witness.
(2) The judge served as a lawyer in the proceeding, or in another
    proceeding involving the same issues, for a party in the present
    proceeding, or gave advice to a party on a matter involved in
    the action — including, within the past two years, a party or
    its lawyer having been a client of the judge or of a lawyer the
    judge practiced with.
(3) Financial interest in the subject matter or in a party,
    including a spouse's or a minor child living in the household's
    interest.
(4) The judge, spouse, or a person within the third degree of
    relationship to either is a party, or an officer, director, or
    trustee of a party.
(5) The judge, spouse, former spouse, child, sibling, or parent of
    the judge or the judge's spouse, or a person associated in
    private practice with such a person, is a lawyer in the
    proceeding.
(6) A person aware of the facts might reasonably entertain a doubt
    that the judge would be able to be impartial — including actual
    bias or prejudice against a party or attorney, or a belief by
    the judge that he or she cannot be fair. This is the broadest
    ground and the hardest to prove: facts, not conclusions, are
    required.
(7)-(8) Other statutory grounds — check the current text of
    Section 170.1(a) before pleading any additional ground.

CAPTION — FILL IN:
[Case name], Case No. [___], Superior Court of California, County
of [___]. Statement of Disqualification of Judge [name] for Cause
(CCP 170.1, 170.3).

VERIFIED STATEMENT — FILL IN, FACT BY FACT:
I, [name], declare under penalty of perjury under the laws of the
State of California that the following is true and correct:
1. Judge [name] is assigned to [proceeding / trial / hearing on
   date].
2. Ground under CCP 170.1(a)(___): [state the subdivision].
3. Facts supporting that ground: [Date, what happened, who was
   present, exact words or rulings, documents — one fact per
   numbered paragraph. Attach exhibits where available. Do NOT
   plead legal conclusions as facts, and do NOT plead facts you
   cannot support.]
4. When I learned these facts: [date — timeliness matters; a
   statement filed after trial or hearing has commenced is treated
   differently, and the challenged judge may order the proceeding
   to continue while another judge decides disqualification].
5. Prior challenges: [State whether you have previously filed a
   statement of disqualification against this judge in this case.
   Generally one statement per judge, unless genuinely new facts
   have emerged since the first.]

WHAT HAPPENS NEXT (CCP 170.3):
- Serve the statement as the statute and local rules require,
  including personal service on the judge or the judge's clerk
  while the judge is in the courthouse where required to start the
  response clock — check the current text.
- Within 10 days after filing or service, whichever is later, the
  challenged judge may file a consent to disqualification or a
  written verified answer admitting or denying the allegations.
  Failure to file either in time is deemed consent.
- If the judge answers and disputes the statement, the judge does
  NOT decide his or her own disqualification — the question goes
  to another judge. The parties have five days to agree on that
  judge; if they cannot agree, the Chairperson of the Judicial
  Council selects one. No peremptory challenge lies against the
  judge chosen to decide the disqualification question.
- The challenged judge may strike the statement, without sending
  it to another judge, only if it is untimely or discloses no legal
  ground for disqualification on its face (CCP 170.4(b)).

PART B — PEREMPTORY CHALLENGE / AFFIDAVIT OF PREJUDICE (CCP 170.6)

DECLARATION — FILL IN:
I, [name, party or attorney], declare under penalty of perjury that
Judge [name] is prejudiced against [party / attorney / interest],
and that [party] cannot have a fair and impartial trial or hearing
before that judge. I move to disqualify Judge [name] under CCP
170.6. No factual proof is required for this challenge.

TIMING — CHECK YOUR ASSIGNMENT TYPE BEFORE FILING (THESE ARE THE
COMMON RULES; THE ASSIGNMENT TYPE CONTROLS):
- If the judge is known at least 10 days before the trial or
  hearing, file at least 5 days before that date.
- All-purpose assignment, criminal: within 10 days after notice of
  the all-purpose assignment (or after appearance, if later).
- All-purpose assignment, civil: within 15 days after notice of
  the assignment (or after appearance, if later).
- After reversal on appeal, if the same trial judge is assigned to
  conduct a new trial: within 60 days after notice of that
  assignment.
- NEVER after trial has commenced: drawing of the first juror's
  name, opening statement in a nonjury trial, or swearing of the
  first witness / giving of evidence.
- ONE per side per case. Co-parties on the same side share one
  challenge. Once used, it is spent; the next assigned judge is
  taken unless a for-cause ground under CCP 170.1 exists.
- Not available at settlement conferences or case management
  conferences, and not against the judge chosen to decide a
  CCP 170.1 disqualification question.

PROOF OF SERVICE: Serve all parties as required, and file proof
of service with the challenge.

SOURCES TO CHECK BEFORE FILING:
- CCP 170.1, 170.3, 170.4, 170.6 — Justia / California Legislative
  Information
- California Rules of Court, and the assigning court's local rules
  (assignment type and service method control the deadline)
