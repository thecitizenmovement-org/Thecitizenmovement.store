YOUR RIGHTS IN JAIL
The Citizen Movement -- thecitizenmovement.org

Being in custody does not erase your rights. Know them, use the
grievance system, and keep copies of everything.

GRIEVANCES -- YOUR PAPER TRAIL

- File a grievance for every problem: conditions, staff conduct,
  medical issues, lost property. If it is not in writing, it did not
  happen.
- Keep your own copy of every grievance with the tracking number on
  it. Keep a copy with someone outside if you can.
- Complete EVERY appeal level, even if the first answer denies you.
  Federal law (the Prison Litigation Reform Act) requires you to
  exhaust the full grievance process BEFORE any lawsuit -- skip a step
  and a court can throw out your case.
- See our free "Custody Facility Grievance" template.

MEDICAL CARE

- You have the right to adequate medical, dental, and mental health
  care. Deliberate indifference to serious medical needs is unlawful.
- Submit sick-call slips IN WRITING and keep copies. Verbal requests
  vanish.
- For emergencies, write EMERGENCY on the grievance and hand it
  directly to the watch commander or medical staff.

LEGAL MAIL

- Mail to and from your attorney is confidential.
- Staff may open it in your presence to check for contraband, but may
  NOT read it.
- Write "LEGAL MAIL -- ATTORNEY-CLIENT PRIVILEGED" clearly on the
  envelope.

COURTS AND LAW LIBRARY

- You have a right of meaningful access to the courts, which includes
  access to law library materials.
- Request law library time in writing if it is being denied.

RETALIATION

- Staff may not punish you for filing grievances or complaints.
- If they do -- cell moves, lost privileges, threats -- document it
  in writing immediately. Retaliation is itself grievable and
  actionable.

PHONE CALLS

- Jail calls are recorded EXCEPT calls to your attorney. Assume every
  other call is heard by the prosecution.
- Do not discuss your case on any non-attorney call.

EMERGENCIES

- Medical emergency or immediate danger: write EMERGENCY at the top,
  hand it directly to staff, and also submit a sick-call slip the
  same day. Keep copies of both.

This fact sheet provides general legal information only -- not legal advice. Consult a licensed attorney for your situation. -- The Citizen Movement, thecitizenmovement.org
