TRIAGING YOUR CASE: WHAT TO FIGHT, WHAT TO DROP
The Citizen Movement -- thecitizenmovement.org

You cannot fight everything at once. Triage is how you decide what
deserves your energy, what needs more work, and what to let go --
before a judge decides for you.

WHAT TRIAGE MEANS

- Think of an emergency room. Doctors treat the life-threatening
  injuries first. They do not spend time on bruises while something
  is bleeding.
- In law, the bleeding is always DEADLINES. A claim with a live
  deadline gets attention first, no matter how strong the others are.
- Triage is not giving up. It is spending your limited time and
  energy where it can actually win.

STEP 1 -- LIST EVERY CLAIM OR ISSUE

- From your timeline, write each separate claim or defense on its own
  line. One line each. Be specific: "officer used excessive force on
  [date]" is a claim; "the police were unfair" is not.
- Include defenses too: "I was not at the location on [date]" is a
  defense line.
- Do not rank them yet. Just get everything on paper.

STEP 2 -- THE FIVE TRIAGE QUESTIONS

Ask these five questions about EVERY line:

- 1. What is the legal basis? Name the law, code section, or right.
  If you cannot name one, that is a warning sign.
- 2. What is the deadline -- and is it already blown? Check the
  timeline sheet. A perfect claim filed one day late is worth nothing.
- 3. What proof do I actually have? Documents, dates, witnesses --
  real things, not feelings.
- 4. What proof am I missing, and can I get it? A CPRA request, a
  witness declaration, a record you can order. If the missing proof
  is obtainable, note exactly how.
- 5. Who decides this -- a judge, a jury, an agency -- and what do
  they care about? Judges care about law and deadlines. Juries care
  about credibility and story. Agencies care about their own rules.

STEP 3 -- SORT INTO THREE PILES

- STRONG: clear legal basis + real proof + live deadline. Lead with
  these. These are your case.
- DEVELOPABLE: good legal basis but thin proof. Work these next.
  Gather the missing documents, send the records requests, get the
  declarations. Each piece of new proof can upgrade a claim.
- WEAK OR DEAD: missed deadline, no proof and none obtainable, or no
  legal basis. Drop these. Arguing them does not help -- it hurts.

THE HARD TRUTH

- One weak claim argued loudly can sink three strong ones. Judges
  stop listening when you waste their time on losers.
- Dropping a dead claim is not weakness. It is what good lawyers do.
  It makes your strong claims stand out.
- If a claim is dead because of a missed deadline, mourn it and move
  on. Do not build your case around reviving it.

DEADLINES FIRST, ALWAYS

- Before doing anything else, calendar every deadline from the
  timeline sheet: the 6-month tort claim, the 10-day CPRA clock, the
  15-day discovery clock, filing dates, hearing dates.
- Work backward from each deadline. If a filing is due Friday, the
  draft is done Wednesday, the copies are made Thursday.
- Set two alarms for every deadline: one a week out, one the day
  before.

RE-TRIAGE MONTHLY

- Cases evolve. New evidence upgrades a claim from DEVELOPABLE to
  STRONG. A missed deadline kills one outright.
- Once a month, re-ask the five questions for every live claim and
  re-sort the piles.
- When something new happens -- a denial letter, a new witness, a
  court ruling -- triage it the same day. Do not let it sit.
