ASSISTANT FILING SHEET — MODIFYING CUSTODY / VISITATION
For the AI assistant to present to a paying customer alongside
explainer-sheet-custody-visitation-modification.txt. General
information only, not legal advice. Use the official Judicial
Council forms — this sheet is not a form.

WHAT THIS IS FOR:
Asking the family court to change an existing child custody or
visitation (parenting time) order — the schedule, legal custody,
physical custody, or both.

WHO FILES IT:
A parent in the existing family-law case, in the court that issued
the current order, using the existing case number.

OFFICIAL ROUTE — DO THIS FIRST:
1. Get FL-300 (Request for Order), FL-311 (Custody/Visitation
   Attachment), and, for the other parent, FL-320 (Responsive
   Declaration) from courts.ca.gov or the court clerk.
2. File FL-300 + FL-311 + supporting declaration in the issuing
   court.
3. Serve the other parent as the FL-300 instructions and local
   rules require — verify the service deadline against your
   court's instructions, do not guess it.
4. Attend Child Custody Recommending Counseling (CCRC) if the
   parents cannot agree, then attend the hearing.

WHAT GOES IN IT — CHECKLIST:
[ ] Existing order: date, court, current legal/physical custody
    and schedule
[ ] Exact new orders wanted (FL-311): legal custody, physical
    custody, weekly schedule, holidays, transportation, exchange
[ ] Changed circumstances since the last order: numbered facts,
    dates, documents, witnesses — tied to the child's welfare
[ ] Best-interest showing under Family Code 3011: health, safety,
    welfare, abuse history, contact with each parent, substance use
[ ] Efforts to resolve without court, and why they did not work
[ ] Declaration signed under penalty of perjury

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER FILING:
- The other parent may file FL-320 in response.
- CCRC is scheduled if custody/visitation is contested.
- At the hearing the judge decides on best interest, and, for a
  final order, on significant change of circumstances as well.
- Only a signed court order changes the existing order — a verbal
  or private agreement does not.

COMMON TRAPS:
- Refiling on facts already decided.
- Asking for a custody-label change on a schedule-only showing,
  or vice versa — match the standard to what you are actually
  asking to change.
- Filing a private agreement and assuming the order changed.
- Skipping CCRC or service — both can delay or defeat the request.
- Filing where abuse, relocation, or a restraining order is
  involved without legal advice.

PAIR WITH (SAME LIBRARY):
- Challenging a ruling: draft-notice-of-appeal.txt.
- Records to support the declaration: CPRA / dispatch-log
  templates where public records are involved.
