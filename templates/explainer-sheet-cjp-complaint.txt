WHAT THE CJP IS:
An independent state agency (California Constitution, article VI,
section 18) that investigates complaints of judicial misconduct or
incapacity and, if appropriate, imposes discipline. It protects the
public, enforces standards of conduct, and maintains confidence in
the judicial system.

WHO IT CAN AND CANNOT HANDLE:
- CAN: all judges of California's superior courts, and justices of
  the Court of Appeal and Supreme Court; also former judges, for
  conduct before retirement or resignation.
- CANNOT: federal judges, workers' compensation judges, judges pro
  tem, or private judges.
- Subordinate judicial officers (court commissioners and referees,
  who are attorneys employed by the court): complain FIRST to the
  court where that officer serves. Only if unsatisfied with the
  court's response may you submit to the Commission, within 30 days
  of the mailing of the court's response.

WHAT COUNTS AS MISCONDUCT — AND WHAT DOES NOT:
- MISCONDUCT: specific behavior by the judicial officer — what the
  judge did and said, on what date, with witnesses and documents.
  Conclusions alone ("the judge was rude" / "the judge was
  biased") are not enough; the Commission needs a full statement
  of facts.
- NOT MISCONDUCT BY ITSELF: an error in a decision or ruling.
  Wrongly excluding evidence, imposing an improper sentence,
  awarding custody or support to the wrong party, incorrectly
  resolving a legal issue, or believing perjured testimony does
  not generally constitute misconduct, and a complaint describing
  only a legal error will not be investigated. Appeal may be the
  only remedy for a ruling error — or there may be no remedy.

WHAT THE CJP CANNOT DO:
- It is NOT an appellate court. It cannot change a judge's decision
  or ruling, and it cannot issue orders in any case, including
  ordering anyone to do anything in your case.
- It can only investigate the complaint and, if appropriate,
  impose discipline on the judicial officer.
- A CJP complaint is therefore NOT a substitute for a
  disqualification statement under CCP 170.1/170.6 (see
  draft-judge-disqualification.txt) or for an appeal (see
  draft-notice-of-appeal.txt). Use each route for what it actually
  does.

WHAT TO PUT IN THE OFFICIAL FORM (CONTENT GUIDE — NOT THE FORM):
1. Full name of the judicial officer, and the court where the
   officer sits.
2. If the complaint involves a court case: case type, case name
   and number, and your relationship to the case.
3. Date or dates the conduct occurred.
4. Detailed description of the action or behavior believed to be
   misconduct — what was done and said, in chronological order,
   with names and telephone numbers of witnesses.
5. Copies (never originals) of court documents, minute orders,
   transcripts, or recordings that show the misconduct — or mention
   them in the complaint if you cannot attach them.
6. No legal analysis or discussion of the canons of the Code of
   Judicial Ethics is necessary. Facts are what is needed.
