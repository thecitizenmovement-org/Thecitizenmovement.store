INFORMAL DISCOVERY REQUEST
California Penal Code section 1054.1

INSTRUCTIONS: Send this letter to the prosecutor's office handling
your case as soon as possible after arraignment. This is the informal
discovery request the law requires BEFORE you can file a formal
motion to compel. Send by a trackable method and keep a copy with
proof of delivery. The prosecution must turn over the listed
materials within 15 days.

    Informal Discovery Request -- Penal Code sections 1054.1, 1054.5(b)

Dear [NAME OR "Deputy District Attorney"]:

Pursuant to Penal Code section 1054.1, I request that the prosecution
disclose all of the following materials and information in its
possession or reasonably available to it, within 15 days as required
by Penal Code section 1054.5(b):

1. POLICE AND INCIDENT REPORTS. All law enforcement reports,
   supplemental reports, and investigative notes relating to this
   case, including reports from every agency involved.

2. WITNESS IDENTITIES AND STATEMENTS. The names and addresses of all
   persons the prosecution intends to call as witnesses, and all
   written or recorded statements of those witnesses.

3. DEFENDANT'S STATEMENTS. All written or recorded statements made by
   me, the defendant, to law enforcement or their agents.

4. EXPERT REPORTS. Any reports, notes, or opinions of experts the
   prosecution intends to call, including the experts' qualifications.

5. CRIMINAL HISTORIES. The criminal history ("rap sheet") of every
   prosecution witness, including pending cases and any deals,
   promises, or inducements offered to any witness.

6. EXCULPATORY EVIDENCE (BRADY MATERIAL). Any and all evidence
   favorable to the defense -- evidence that tends to show I am not
   guilty, to impeach prosecution witnesses, or to mitigate punishment
   -- as required by Brady v. Maryland and its progeny. This is a
   continuing obligation.

7. AUDIO, VIDEO, AND PHOTOGRAPHS. All audio recordings, video
   recordings (including body-worn camera, dash camera, and
   surveillance footage), and photographs related to the investigation
   or arrest.

8. 911 CALLS AND DISPATCH RECORDS. All 911 recordings, dispatch logs,
   and CAD (computer-aided dispatch) records relating to this incident.

9. PHYSICAL EVIDENCE. A list and description of all physical or real
   evidence obtained, and reasonable access to inspect it.

10. SEARCH AND SEIZURE DOCUMENTS. Copies of all search warrants,
    warrant applications, affidavits, and inventory returns.

11. IDENTIFICATION PROCEDURES. Records of any lineup, photo array, or
    show-up identification procedures, including who conducted them
    and who was present.

12. OTHER DISCOVERABLE MATERIAL. Anything else the prosecution is
    required to disclose under Penal Code section 1054.1 or the
    United States and California Constitutions.

RESERVATION OF RIGHTS

If the prosecution fails to comply within 15 days, I reserve the
right to file a formal motion to compel discovery under Penal Code
section 1054.5(b), and to seek any other appropriate relief,
including sanctions and exclusion of undisclosed evidence.

Sincerely,

________________________________
[YOUR FULL NAME -- OR ATTORNEY NAME AND BAR NUMBER]

FILING NOTES: This is a letter to the prosecutor, not a court filing
-- but keep proof you sent it (certified mail receipt or email
confirmation), because you must show the court you made this informal
request before filing a motion to compel. Send it to the district
attorney's office in the county prosecuting your case, addressed to
the assigned deputy if you know the name. If new evidence surfaces
later, the prosecution's duty to disclose is continuing -- you may
send a supplemental request. If the DA refuses or ignores the
request, the next step is a noticed motion to compel filed with the
court, with a copy of this letter attached as an exhibit.
