COMPLAINT FOR VIOLATION OF CIVIL RIGHTS
42 U.S.C. SECTION 1983 — FEDERAL COURT, PRO SE OUTLINE
THE CITIZEN MOVEMENT — GENERAL LEGAL INFORMATION ONLY, NOT LEGAL ADVICE

INSTRUCTIONS: This is a drafting outline, not a court form. Federal
courts use their own pro se complaint form (U.S. Courts Form Pro Se 15,
Complaint for Violation of Civil Rights, for non-prisoners; a different
form applies to prisoners). Use the court's form where one is required
and use this outline to organize the facts and claims that go in it.
Check the local rules of the U.S. District Court where you file, the
filing fee or in forma pauperis application (28 U.S.C. 1915), and the
statute of limitations in your state — in California, Section 1983
claims borrow the state's personal-injury limitations period. Consult
a licensed attorney before filing. Do not file claims you cannot
support with specific facts.

STATUTE: 42 U.S.C. 1983 creates a civil action against any person who,
under color of state law, deprives another person of rights secured by
the Constitution or laws of the United States. It creates no rights
of its own — it enforces rights found elsewhere, such as the Fourth,
First, Eighth, or Fourteenth Amendments.

ELEMENTS YOU MUST PLEAD WITH SPECIFIC FACTS (NOT CONCLUSIONS):
1. STATE ACTION / UNDER COLOR OF STATE LAW
   - Name each defendant, and state the government job, agency, and
     authority they were acting under at the time.
   - An officer using force during an arrest is still acting under
     color of state law even if no policy authorized the force.
   - Purely private conduct, and a public defender acting as defense
     counsel, are generally NOT state action for Section 1983.
2. DEPRIVATION OF A SPECIFIC FEDERAL RIGHT
   - Name the specific constitutional provision: e.g., Fourth
     Amendment unreasonable search/seizure or excessive force; First
     Amendment retaliation for protected speech; Eighth Amendment
     cruel conditions; Fourteenth Amendment due process.
   - Vague "unfair treatment" is not enough.
3. PERSONAL INVOLVEMENT AND CAUSATION
   - State what each defendant personally did, on what date, where,
     and how that act caused your injury. Supervisors are not liable
     merely because they supervised someone else.
4. INJURY AND RELIEF REQUESTED
   - State the injury (physical, property, loss of liberty, etc.),
     with medical records, photos, reports, or other proof listed.
   - State the relief: compensatory damages, punitive damages where
     allowed, declaratory relief, injunctive relief, costs and fees
     (42 U.S.C. 1988 allows fees to a prevailing plaintiff).

CAPTION AND PARTIES — FILL IN:
[Your full name], Plaintiff, v. [Defendant name(s), title, agency],
Defendant(s). United States District Court for the [___ District of
California]. Jurisdiction: 28 U.S.C. 1331 (federal question) and
42 U.S.C. 1983. Venue: the district where a defendant resides or
where the events occurred (28 U.S.C. 1391).

STATEMENT OF FACTS — FILL IN, IN CHRONOLOGICAL ORDER:
On [date], at [place], [defendant] did [specific act]. [Continue
with each event, one paragraph per event, with dates, places, and
witnesses.] Attach or list exhibits: police reports, video, medical
records, photographs, correspondence. Number each paragraph.

CLAIMS FOR RELIEF — FILL IN:
Count 1: Violation of [Amendment] under 42 U.S.C. 1983, against
[defendant(s)], incorporating the facts above.
[Repeat for each separate constitutional claim and defendant.]

PRAYER FOR RELIEF:
Wherefore, Plaintiff prays for judgment as stated above, plus such
other relief as the court deems just.

VERIFICATION AND SIGNATURE:
I declare under penalty of perjury that the foregoing is true and
correct. Signed: [Name, date, address, phone, email].

SOURCES TO CHECK BEFORE FILING:

- U.S. Courts pro se complaint forms — uscourts.gov
- Local rules of the district court where you file
