MOTION TO REDUCE BAIL / FOR RELEASE ON LESS RESTRICTIVE CONDITIONS
CALIFORNIA PENAL CODE SECTIONS 1275, 1289; IN RE HUMPHREY (2021)
THE CITIZEN MOVEMENT — GENERAL LEGAL INFORMATION ONLY, NOT LEGAL ADVICE

INSTRUCTIONS: Bail is set, reduced, or denied on the factors in
Penal Code 1275, with public safety as the primary consideration.
After In re Humphrey (2021) 11 Cal.5th 135, a court also must
consider ability to pay and less restrictive alternatives — bail
may not be set so high that it effectively denies release solely
because the defendant cannot pay. A motion under Penal Code 1289
asks the court, after arraignment, to reconsider bail. File with a
declaration, serve the People, and be ready to address public
safety first — a motion that only says "I cannot afford it" without
addressing safety, seriousness, record, and appearance will fail.
Check the criminal court's local rules. Consult a licensed attorney,
especially for serious or violent felonies, where release conditions
are stricter.

STATUTE: Penal Code 1275(a) — in setting, reducing, or denying
bail, the judge or magistrate shall take into consideration the
protection of the public, the seriousness of the offense charged,
the previous criminal record of the defendant, and the probability
of appearing at trial or hearing. Public safety shall be the
primary consideration. In considering seriousness, the court
includes alleged injury to the victim, alleged threats to a victim
or witness, alleged use of a firearm or other deadly weapon, and
alleged use or possession of controlled substances.

CAPTION — FILL IN:
People of the State of California v. [Defendant name], Case No.
[___], Superior Court of California, County of [___].
Motion to Reduce Bail (Pen. Code 1289; 1275).

MOTION — FILL IN:
Current bail: $[___], set on [date], [cash / bond / own
recognizance denied]. Defendant asks the court to reduce bail to
$[___] or release on [own recognizance / supervised release /
other less restrictive conditions: ___].

DECLARATION IN SUPPORT — FILL IN, WITH FACTS NOT CONCLUSIONS:
1. Public safety: [Facts showing no danger — no threats, no weapon
   allegation, no victim contact, proposed conditions such as
   no-contact order, stay-away, supervision, surrender of passport
   or firearms where applicable.]
2. Seriousness: [Address the charge honestly — nature of offense,
   alleged injury or lack of it, and why the current amount is
   disproportionate to the charge as alleged.]
3. Criminal record: [Record, or lack of it; prior failures to appear,
   if any, addressed honestly with an explanation.]
4. Probability of appearing: [Community ties — years in area,
   family, employment, housing, prior court appearances kept.]
5. Ability to pay (Humphrey): [Income, assets, debts, dependents,
   and what amount, if any, the defendant can actually post without
   severe hardship to basic needs — rent, food, medical care.
   Attach a financial declaration if the court requires one.]
6. Less restrictive alternatives: [Supervision, check-ins, travel
   restrictions, or other conditions that would assure appearance
   and safety at a lower or no cash amount.]

PRAYER:
Defendant prays that bail be reduced to $[___] or that defendant be
released on the less restrictive conditions above, and for such
other relief as is just. Proof of service on the People attached.

SOURCES TO CHECK BEFORE FILING:
- Penal Code 1275, 1289 — Justia / California Legislative Information
- In re Humphrey (2021) 11 Cal.5th 135 (ability to pay; less
  restrictive alternatives)
