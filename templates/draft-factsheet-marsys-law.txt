MARSY'S LAW: YOUR RIGHTS AS A CRIME VICTIM IN CALIFORNIA
California Constitution, Article I, section 28
The Citizen Movement -- thecitizenmovement.org

If someone committed a crime against you, California law gives you
specific rights in the criminal process. These are your rights -- use
them.

YOU HAVE THE RIGHT TO

- Be treated with fairness, dignity, and respect throughout the
  process.
- Be notified of all court proceedings, including bail hearings,
  sentencings, and parole hearings.
- Be present at proceedings and to be heard -- at sentencing, at
  parole hearings, and at any proceeding involving release or plea
  decisions.
- Confer with the prosecutor about the case, including before charges
  are filed or a plea deal is offered.
- Refuse interview or deposition requests from the defense. You do not
  have to talk to the defendant's lawyer or investigator.
- Protection from the defendant -- the court can issue stay-away and
  no-contact orders.
- A speedy disposition of the case without unnecessary delay.
- Have your safety considered when bail or release conditions are set.

RESTITUTION -- MAKING YOU WHOLE

- You have the right to restitution: money the offender pays for your
  losses.
- It covers medical and counseling bills, lost wages, stolen or
  damaged property, and other direct losses from the crime.
- Tell the prosecutor about every loss and keep receipts, bills, and
  pay stubs. The judge cannot order what is not documented.
- Ask the prosecutor to request a restitution hearing if your losses
  are disputed.

IF YOUR RIGHTS ARE VIOLATED

- Contact the victim advocate at the district attorney's office --
  every county DA has victim services.
- You can also contact the California Victim Compensation Board for
  help with crime-related costs.
- Put complaints in writing and keep copies.

NOTE FOR WITNESSES

- These rights belong to victims of crime. Witnesses who are not
  victims have fewer rights, but you still have the right to be
  treated with dignity, to be protected from intimidation, and to
  seek a subpoena or witness fee information from the court.
